\begin{abstract}
\noindent This report characterizes selected small-language-model configurations on one NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti (4 GiB VRAM) host. It combines historical single-host throughput and memory observations, bounded inference campaigns, analytical resource estimates, and exploratory behavioral probes. Repeated queries within one hardware run are descriptive observations, not independent hardware replicates; previously reported query-level $t$ tests and effect sizes do not support hardware-level inference and are not interpreted here. In the measured Gemma-4 configuration contrast, IQ4\_XS and MTP were associated with higher observed decode throughput (+28.2\% and +25.6\%, respectively), while the combined configuration differed by +61.0\% from baseline; these are descriptive contrasts for the recorded setup. A 20-item GSM8K subset is likewise exploratory. The accompanying viability assessment classifies each studied configuration by everyday, constrained-local, and production scenarios. Evidence supports only bounded research feasibility for some exact configurations; everyday-use quality and production readiness remain insufficiently established. No claim of guaranteed privacy, safety, latency, or service availability is made.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Small Language Models (SLMs); Transformer Architecture; Key-Value Cache; Grouped-Query Attention (GQA); Multi-Token Prediction; Roofline Model; Defensive Safety.

\section{Introduction and Contextualization}

Edge execution of Small Language Models (SLMs, parameterized in the 0.5B to 4B range) can reduce dependence on remote inference services. Local execution alone does not guarantee data sovereignty, bounded latency, or resilience; these properties depend on the complete system configuration and its operation. However, local autonomy directly confronts rigid hardware boundaries: on commodity accelerators constrained to a $4096\text{ MiB}$ (4 GiB) physical memory budget, such as the NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti, headroom for static tensor allocations, driver runtime contexts, and dynamic inference buffers is extraordinarily constrained.

The theoretical baseline for understanding contemporary language models begins with the seminal work \emph{``Attention Is All You Need''} by Vaswani et al. (2017)~\cite{vaswani2017}. By demonstrating that recurrence and convolutions could be entirely replaced with Scaled Dot-Product Attention, Vaswani et al. enabled massive training parallelism across sequence dimensions. Nonetheless, modern edge SLMs incorporate fundamental structural refinements absent from the original 2017 formulation: decoder-only autoregressive topologies, Grouped-Query Attention (GQA)~\cite{ainslie2023}, Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE)~\cite{su2024}, Root Mean Square Layer Normalization (RMSNorm)~\cite{zhang2019}, SwiGLU gated activations~\cite{shazeer2020}, and speculative decoding via Multi-Token Prediction (MTP)~\cite{gloeckle2024mtp}, distinct from classical draft-model speculative decoding~\cite{leviathan2023}. Furthermore, advanced quantization schemes and importance-matrix calibrations implemented in \emph{llama.cpp} (k-quants and IQ formats)~\cite{gerganov2023llamacpp,dettmers2022} make billion-parameter model deployment physically feasible on consumer-grade silicon.

This technical report delivers an empirical and theoretical investigation of SLM inference under physical VRAM constraints. We evaluate eight model configurations on an isolated physical node running an NVIDIA GTX 1050 Ti GPU under \emph{llama.cpp}. The primary contributions of this work are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{Mathematical Formalization of Edge Transformer Mechanisms:} We trace the exact mathematical lineage of Attention, KV caching, GQA compression ratios, and Roofline arithmetic intensity limits.
    \item \textbf{Three-Condition Ablation of Quantization and Speculative Decoding:} In a three-condition, partial ablation on Gemma-4-E2B, we compare quantization gains ($C_2$ vs $C_1$, $+28.2\%$) and the associated difference with Multi-Token Prediction ($C_3$ vs $C_2$, $+25.6\%$); this is not a complete $2 \times 2$ factorial design.
    \item \textbf{Analytical Bandwidth Demand Modeling and Thermal Telemetry:} We establish an analytical bandwidth demand proxy ($B_{\text{demanda}} = S_{\text{pesos}} \times \text{Throughput}$), correcting historical overestimations, and audit the active $311.58\text{ s}$ campaign, measuring die heating ($44^\circ\text{C} \to 74^\circ\text{C}$) and GPU Boost clock throttling ($-5.2\%$).
    \item \textbf{Multilingual Tokenization and Context Headroom Audit:} Using a standardized trilingual parallel reference corpus (en-US, pt-BR, zh-CN), we measure subword fertility and context degradation across vocabulary configurations.
    \item \textbf{Exploratory Single-Turn Defensive Safety Evaluation:} We assess robustness across ten canary probes, formalizing a three-stage taxonomy for tool-calling security (proposed, valid, executed) and cataloging sensitive topics evaluation protocols.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Architectural Evolution of Transformer Decoders:} Vaswani et al. (2017)~\cite{vaswani2017} introduced the encoder-decoder attention architecture. Subsequent foundational models established the supremacy of decoder-only configurations for autoregressive text generation. To mitigate quadratic memory overhead in self-attention, Shazeer (2019)~\cite{shazeer2019} proposed Multi-Query Attention (MQA), collapsing key and value heads to unity. Ainslie et al. (2023)~\cite{ainslie2023} generalized this into Grouped-Query Attention (GQA), interpolating between MHA expressivity and MQA memory efficiency. Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) by Su et al. (2024)~\cite{su2024} replaced additive sinusoidal embeddings with complex space rotations, providing relative distance awareness.

\textbf{Speculative Decoding and Multi-Token Prediction:} Speculative execution in language models was formalized by Leviathan et al. (2023)~\cite{leviathan2023}, employing an independent, smaller draft model to generate candidate token sequences verified in parallel by a larger target model. Gloeckle et al. (2024)~\cite{gloeckle2024mtp} developed Multi-Token Prediction (MTP), integrating auxiliary prediction heads directly into the shared trunk representation, avoiding the operational overhead of serving two distinct neural networks.

\textbf{Edge Serving and Hardware Profiling:} The Roofline performance model formulated by Williams et al. (2009)~\cite{williams2009} serves as the canonical framework for diagnosing memory-bound versus compute-bound execution regimes. Gerganov et al. (2023)~\cite{gerganov2023llamacpp} engineered \emph{llama.cpp}, introducing k-quants and importance-matrix quantization (IQ) based on second-order error minimization~\cite{dettmers2022}.

\textbf{Multilingual Inequities and Tokenization:} Petrov et al. (2023)~\cite{petrov2023} and Ahia et al. (2023)~\cite{ahia2023} demonstrated that Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) tokenizers trained predominantly on English text impose severe subword fragmentation on non-English scripts, inflating processing latency and operational costs.

\textbf{Defensive Safety and Agentic Vulnerabilities:} R\"ottger et al. (2024)~\cite{rottger2023} established the XSTest benchmark to systematically detect exaggerated safety behaviors (over-refusal) in aligned models. Cheng et al. (2024)~\cite{cheng2024} (BIPIA) and Debenedetti et al. (2024)~\cite{debenedetti2024} (AgentDojo) demonstrated that edge SLMs remain vulnerable to indirect instruction injection and agentic tool manipulation, requiring rigid deterministic sandbox layers.

\section{Architectural Foundations and Hardware Modeling}

\subsection{Mathematical Foundations: Attention, MHA, and GQA}

The core mathematical primitive is Scaled Dot-Product Attention. Given queries ($Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_k}$), keys ($K \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_k}$), and values ($V \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_v}$):
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \operatorname{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V
\label{eq:scaled_dot_product}
\end{equation}
The scaling factor $\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}$ preserves unit variance when query and key elements are independent zero-mean unit-variance variables, preventing the softmax function from saturating into regions with vanishing gradients.

In Multi-Head Attention (MHA), linear projections map inputs into $h$ distinct representation subspaces:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MHA}(Q,K,V) = \operatorname{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_h)W^O
\end{equation}
where $\text{head}_i = \operatorname{Attention}(QW_i^Q, KW_i^K, VW_i^V)$.

In autoregressive decoding, past key and value tensors are preserved in VRAM within the Key-Value (KV) cache. The total physical VRAM demanded by the KV cache in FP16 precision ($2\text{ bytes}$ per element) scales strictly with sequence length $L$:
\begin{equation}
\text{VRAM}_{\text{KV}} = 2 \times n_{\text{layers}} \times n_{\text{kv}} \times d_{\text{head}} \times L \times 2\text{ bytes}
\label{eq:kv_cache_formula}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:kv_cache_formula} clarifies the impact of GQA. In Llama-3.2-3B ($n_{\text{layers}} = 28$, $d_{\text{head}} = 128$), an MHA configuration ($n_{\text{kv}} = 24$) would demand $1342\text{ MiB}$ for $L = 4096$, whereas its actual GQA design ($n_{\text{kv}} = 8$, $3:1$ ratio) consumes only $448\text{ MiB}$. This saving of nearly $900\text{ MiB}$ allows the model's static weights ($2120\text{ MiB}$) and driver context ($256\text{ MiB}$) to coexist within the $4096\text{ MiB}$ hardware envelope without out-of-memory crashes.

\subsection{Roofline Model and Pascal GP107 Hardware Constraints}

Hardware execution bounds are formalized via the Roofline model~\cite{williams2009}. The testbed GPU is an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti (Pascal GP107, 14 nm FinFET, Compute Capability 6.1, specifications documented in~\cite{nvidia2016pascal}). The GPU features 768 CUDA FP32 cores across 6 Streaming Multiprocessors (SMs), operating at a boost clock of $1392\text{ MHz}$, delivering peak FP32 throughput:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
P_{\text{FP32}} &= 768 \times 2 \times 1.392\text{ GHz}\\
&\approx 2.138\text{ TFLOPS} = 2138\text{ GFLOPS}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Three physical hardware characteristics dictate the constraints observed in this work:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{Absence of Tensor Cores:} Pascal GP107 contains zero hardware matrix acceleration cores (introduced in Volta GV100 and Turing TU102). All matrix multiplications execute on scalar FP32 ALUs.
    \item \textbf{FP16 Rate Degradation:} Unlike server-class Pascal (GP100), GP107 executes FP16 at a degraded $1:64$ rate relative to FP32. Inference engines like \emph{llama.cpp} unpack quantized integer weights directly into FP32 registers for accumulation.
    \item \textbf{DRAM Memory Bandwidth Ceiling:} The board hosts 4096 MiB GDDR5 memory across a 128-bit bus operating at $3504\text{ MHz}$ ($7.008\text{ Gbps}$ effective), yielding theoretical peak bandwidth:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    B_{\text{theor}} &= \frac{128\text{ bits}}{8\text{ bits/byte}} \times 7.008\text{ GHz}\\
    &\approx 112.13\text{ GB/s}
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

The critical arithmetic intensity threshold ($I^*$) defining the operational transition on the Roofline chart is:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 I^* &= \frac{P_{\text{FP32}}}{B_{\text{theor}}}\\
 &= \frac{2138\text{ GFLOPS}}{112.0\text{ GB/s}} \approx 19.1\text{ FLOP/byte}
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Under the assumptions above, the analytical estimate $I_{\text{decode}} \approx 3.6\text{ FLOP/byte} \ll I^* = 19.1$ predicts a memory-bandwidth-limited regime for batch-1 autoregressive decoding on this GTX 1050 Ti. This is a model-based prediction, not a direct measurement of DRAM traffic, and should not be generalized to other GPUs.

\section{Materials and Experimental Methodology}

\subsection{Physical Testbed Instrumentation}

Experiments were conducted on a dedicated bare-metal workstation: AMD Ryzen 5 3600 CPU (6 cores, 12 threads), 32 GiB dual-channel DDR4-3200 memory, and an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti GPU (4096 MiB GDDR5, Compute Capability 6.1). The software stack comprises Debian 12 (Linux kernel 6.8), NVIDIA proprietary driver 550.120, and \texttt{llama-server} compiled from \emph{llama.cpp} (b4120+) with CUDA acceleration. Real-time telemetry was captured at $250\text{ ms}$ intervals via NVML (\emph{NVIDIA Management Library}), recording allocated VRAM and die junction temperature.

\subsection{Experimental Protocols and Datasets}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{Performance Benchmark Matrix ($N=10$):} All performance trials executed under deterministic greedy sampling ($\tau = 0.0$, \texttt{seed=42}) with repetition penalty 1.05. Each model completed 10 sequential iterations following warmup queries. Generated token sequences were strictly identical across repetitions.
    \item \textbf{Trilingual Parallel Corpus (en-US, pt-BR, zh-CN):} A standardized parallel corpus of 12 semantically equivalent texts spanning six functional domains (system commands, academic abstracts, literature, daily communication, user inquiries, programming) was tokenized across vocabularies to quantify subword expansion.
    \item \textbf{Exploratory Single-Turn Defensive Safety Suite ($N=10$):} Ten canary prompts evaluated benign over-refusal ($n=4$), indirect prompt injection ($n=2$), command delimiter isolation ($n=2$), and agentic tool-calling guardrails ($n=2$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Paired GSM8K Evaluation}

We evaluated Qwen3-1.7B in Q4\_K\_M using the GSM8K test split (task version 3) through lm-evaluation-harness 0.4.14.dev0 and llama.cpp build b12024. The two conditions used zero or five demonstrations sampled from the training split, with the same 20 test items, seed 42, batch size 1, greedy decoding (temperature 0), and a 384-token generation limit. The harness's strict-match and flexible-extract exact-match filters were retained. Captured API outputs agreed with all 20 harness predictions in each condition; aggregate metrics were recomputed from the per-sample records. The sanitized aggregate and audit counts are published in \\texttt{results/diagnostics/official-gsm8k-20.json}.

\subsection{Statistical Scope and Experimental Unit}

Performance trials are repeated observations from one host and one recorded run per configuration. They support descriptive summaries of those observations only; they do not provide independent device- or run-level replication. Accordingly, query-level Welch tests, Mann--Whitney tests, confidence intervals interpreted as cross-run uncertainty, and standardized effect sizes are not used to claim hardware-level effects. Future inference requires independently repeated, randomized runs across the intended experimental units.

\subsection{Experimental Duration, Temporal Profiling, and Three-Tier Protocol}

Benchmarking instrumentation uses monotonic clocks (\texttt{time.monotonic()}) to isolate tensor loading, warmup, prefill, decode, and inter-trial cooldown durations. Automated safety guardrails abort execution if die temperature reaches $\ge 75^\circ\text{C}$, memory usage exceeds $\ge 95\%$, or consecutive errors reach $\ge 3$.

We formalize a three-tier experimental protocol:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{Level A (Fast Smoke Test):} Target duration $< 30\text{ s}$ ($1$ trial, short prompt, no extensive warmup). Validates socket connectivity, driver compatibility, and VRAM fitting.
    \item \textbf{Level B (Interleaved Comparative Benchmark):} Target duration $300\text{--}600\text{ s}$ ($N \ge 10$ trials, mandatory warmup, randomized model execution order, and $5\text{--}10\text{ s}$ cooldown). Mitigates thermal accumulation bias and separates cold-start latency from steady-state throughput.
    \item \textbf{Level C (Sustained Temporal Stability and Thermal Stress):} Target duration $900\text{--}1800\text{ s}$ ($15\text{--}30\text{ min}$ continuous run, $N \ge 50$ trials, rolling window metrics). Tracks asymptotic thermal equilibrium, DVFS throttling, and long-session throughput stability.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Roofline Operating Point and Analytical Bandwidth Demand Proxy}

Autoregressive inference memory traffic with unit batch size is modeled via the analytical memory bandwidth demand proxy:
\begin{equation}
B_{\text{demand}} = S_{\text{weights}} \times \text{Throughput}_{\text{decode}}
\end{equation}
where $S_{\text{weights}}$ is the static tensor footprint in gigabytes read from DRAM per generation step. We emphasize that $B_{\text{demand}}$ represents an \emph{analytical bandwidth demand proxy} derived from the Roofline framework, not a physical hardware counter reading of DRAM bus transactions. Under MTP speculative decoding (Gemma-4-E2B), base model weights are streamed once for multiple accepted tokens per step, while auxiliary Q8\_0 draft heads require additional read and verification passes.

Using exact GGUF tensor sizes, we observe:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{Gemma-4-E2B [MTP]:} With $1.737\text{ GB}$ static tensor footprint and $43.35\text{ tok/s}$ throughput, the analytical demand proxy is estimated at $75.3\text{ GB/s}$, or $67.2\%$ of the theoretical peak ($112.0\text{ GB/s}$). This ratio is not measured bus utilization; physical DRAM traffic was not instrumented. Multi-token generation may change effective arithmetic intensity, but that effect was not separately measured here.
    \item \textbf{Llama-3.2-3B:} Allocating $2.019\text{ GB}$ of tensors in Q4\_K\_M at $27.59\text{ tok/s}$, the calculated analytical demand proxy is $55.7\text{ GB/s}$ ($49.7\%$).\hfil
    \item \textbf{Qwen-3.5-2B:} With $1.396\text{ GB}$ of tensors at $37.60\text{ tok/s}$, demand is $52.5\text{ GB/s}$ ($46.9\%$).
    \item \textbf{SmolLM2-1.7B:} Allocating $1.058\text{ GB}$ weights ($1009.1\text{ MiB}$) at $47.80\text{ tok/s}$, analytical demand is $50.6\text{ GB/s}$ ($45.2\%$). This resolves earlier artifacts where generic VRAM deduction over-estimated weights at $2.59\text{ GB}$, producing an impossible $123.8\text{ GB/s}$ that exceeded physical hardware capacity.
    \item \textbf{Qwen-3.5-4B:} With $2.578\text{ GB}$ weights at $18.55\text{ tok/s}$, demand is $47.8\text{ GB/s}$ ($42.7\%$).
    \item \textbf{Qwen-2.5-0.5B:} Allocating $0.469\text{ GB}$ weights ($469.2\text{ MiB}$) at $97.47\text{ tok/s}$, the estimated proxy is $45.7\text{ GB/s}$ ($40.8\%$). These arithmetic estimates do not establish actual bus saturation or utilization.
\end{itemize}

\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}

\subsection{Internal Validity}

Hardware telemetry logged VRAM allocation and die temperature at $250\text{ ms}$ intervals. No direct power measurement was collected, so energy efficiency was not measured. The 10 sequential iterations are within-run repeated observations, not independent hardware replicates.

\subsection{External Validity}

Our findings are established on a single edge GPU architecture (NVIDIA Pascal GP107). Modern accelerators featuring Tensor Cores (Turing, Ampere, Ada Lovelace) or unified memory (Apple Silicon) exhibit different memory-compute trade-offs. Safety findings are based on an exploratory battery of ten probes; broad behavioral generalizations across adversarial distributions require expanded evaluation suites.

\subsection{Construct Validity}

We utilize $B_{\text{demand}} = S_{\text{weights}} \times \text{Throughput}$ as an analytical Roofline demand proxy, which models required memory transfer under single-batch decode but does not capture physical DRAM transactions or L2 cache hit ratios directly. Cold-start Trial 1 latency inflates mean TTFT and prefill standard deviations, which we mitigate by reporting medians and separating startup from steady-state regimes.

\subsection{GSM8K Evaluation Limitations}

The GSM8K comparison covers only 20 test items for one quantized model and one decoding setup; estimates therefore have substantial sampling uncertainty and do not establish general performance across the full dataset or other models. Paired tests use the shared items, but the two filters are related and the unadjusted $p$-values are exploratory. Strict-match failures also include formatting failures (20/20 zero-shot; 6/20 five-shot), while flexible extraction is permissive; neither score alone isolates mathematical reasoning. Two outputs reached the generation-length limit (zero-shot: 2; five-shot: 1), which may truncate reasoning.

The planned 100-pair extension was not completed, so no results from it are reported. This evaluation supports only a within-sample comparison; larger, preregistered evaluations and independent replications are needed.

\subsection{Exploratory HotpotQA Support-Order Pilot}

A source-pinned HotpotQA \texttt{distractor} validation pilot compared support passages placed first versus last in the ten supplied candidate passages. Only 15 of 100 planned paired questions were completed (one normalized answer exact match in each condition; joint answer-and-support exact was 0/15 in both). Input token counts were matched. This is an incomplete descriptive pilot: it does not establish an ordering effect, evaluate a live retriever, or test conflicting document versions. The historical production-recovery verifier initially failed its real-completion check; a later read-only check on 2026-11-07 passed for then-production generation 347 with zero active leases. This check and the old GGUF-path observation are historical only: deployment and alias mappings may have changed, the path may be obsolete, and its absence does not establish a current production failure. The current deployed route/model mapping could not be verified in this environment. No additional GPU batch followed. Per-item methods, limitations, hashes, and raw artifacts are recorded in \url{../../docs/benchmarks/hotpotqa-support-order-partial.md}. Evidence reporting followed traceability and exploratory-versus-confirmatory principles~\cite{kassis2026skills}; that procedural guidance is not empirical support for these results.

\subsection{Model Viability by Deployment Scenario}

The classifications apply only to exact artifacts and assumptions in \texttt{docs/research/model-viability.md}; they are not a general model ranking. For everyday use, evidence is insufficient for every configuration because representative task quality, factuality, and reliability criteria have not been established. On the recorded GTX 1050 Ti 4-GiB/Ryzen 5 3600 testbed, the four primary measured configurations and Qwen3-1.7B are viable with restrictions only as bounded local research or benchmark subjects—not as validated assistants or concurrent services. Historical Qwen2.5-0.5B, SmolLM2-1.7B, and Gemma comparison artifacts have insufficient evidence for local deployment. Production evidence is insufficient for all configurations: current routing, access controls, SLOs, monitoring, incident response, and recovery were not verified. Licensing, privacy, operating costs, and behavior under representative load also require configuration-specific verification.

\section{Conclusions}

This research conducted an empirical investigation of Small Language Model serving under physical memory constraints ($\le 4096\text{ MiB}$ VRAM) on an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti GPU:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{Descriptive ablation contrasts:} In the recorded Gemma-4 configuration, IQ4\_XS and MTP were associated with higher observed throughput (+28.2\% and +25.6\%, respectively); the combined configuration differed by +61.0\% from baseline, with a recorded net VRAM increase of $+40.0\text{ MiB}$. Repeated queries are not independent hardware replicates, so these contrasts do not support hardware-level statistical inference.
    \item \textbf{Roofline estimate:} Under the stated assumptions, the analytical model predicts a memory-bandwidth-limited batch-1 decode regime on this GTX 1050 Ti. The estimated bandwidth demand proxy reaches $75.3\text{ GB/s}$ ($67.2\%$ of theoretical bandwidth) under MTP; physical DRAM traffic was not measured.
    \item \textbf{Experimental Duration and Thermal Drift:} Auditing the active $311.58\text{ s}$ campaign quantified die heating ($44^\circ\text{C} \to 74^\circ\text{C}$) and GPU Boost 3.0 clock throttling ($-5.2\%$), motivating our standardized three-tier protocol (Level A/B/C).
    \item \textbf{Tokenization observations:} In the measured 12-text parallel corpus, the tested SmolLM2 tokenizer used up to $2.4\times$ as many Portuguese and $4.6\times$ as many Chinese tokens as English. This corpus-specific ratio does not establish universal language or model behavior.
    \item \textbf{Exploratory Defensive Probes:} The small canary set is not evidence of general safety or operational viability. Tool execution requires independently enforced application-layer controls; model behavior alone is not a security boundary.
    \item \textbf{GSM8K Few-Shot Observation:} On the same 20 items, five-shot accuracy exceeded zero-shot by 35 percentage points (strict) and 40 points (flexible). This is a descriptive result for this model/configuration and small subset, not a general or confirmatory claim.
\end{enumerate}
