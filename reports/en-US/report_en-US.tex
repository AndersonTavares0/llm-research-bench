\begin{abstract}
Local on-device inference using Small Language Models (SLMs, 2B--4B parameters) provides verifiable data sovereignty and deterministic latency for autonomous edge nodes and private servers. However, operating under severe physical memory limitations ($\le 4096$ MiB VRAM) imposes strict trade-offs across quantization precision, speculative execution, effective memory bandwidth, and safety alignment. This study presents a controlled empirical investigation conducted on an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti GPU (4096 MiB GDDR5, 128-bit memory bus, $112\text{ GB/s}$ theoretical bandwidth, Compute Capability 6.1) hosted in a Proxmox VE LXC container (CT 108). Four candidate models (\textbf{Gemma-4-E2B-it}, \textbf{Qwen-3.5-4B}, \textbf{Qwen-3.5-2B}, and \textbf{Llama-3.2-3B}) were benchmarked under the \texttt{llama-server} runtime (commit b4120+). Telemetry was captured directly via \texttt{libnvidia-ml.so.1} at 250\,ms intervals. A controlled Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) speculative decoding ablation with $N=15$ repetitions isolated a statistically significant \textbf{30.9\%} overall throughput gain ($42.21 \pm 5.99$ vs $32.25 \pm 3.23$ tok/s, 95\% CI, Welch's $t=3.139$, $p=0.0024$, Cohen's $d=1.146$) and \textbf{33.2\%} in steady-state warmed execution ($44.95$ vs $33.75$ tok/s), requiring only 2038 MiB VRAM. Controlled quantization of Gemma-4-E2B revealed that IQ4\_XS delivers a 59.3\% throughput advantage over Q4\_K\_M in steady-state ($41.92$ vs $26.34$ tok/s, $t=27.20$, $p=3.59\times 10^{-9}$). In safety batteries, all models cleanly refused direct exploits (100\% refusal against CVE-2021-44228, stealth keyloggers, and DAN), but universally succumbed to indirect prompt injection embedded in system logs (0\% resistance), proving that current alignment mechanisms fail to isolate untrusted context data.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Systems Theoretical Framework}

Deploying deep neural language models on edge and homelab infrastructure necessitates executing inference without cloud egress. On consumer GPUs such as the NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti (4096 MiB GDDR5, 768 CUDA cores, $B_{\text{theor}} = 112.0\text{ GB/s}$, 75W TDP), memory bandwidth is the primary operational constraint during autoregressive decoding.

\subsection{The Roofline Model for Pascal Microarchitecture}
Inference efficiency is governed by the operational arithmetic intensity $I$, defined as the ratio of floating-point operations performed per byte of DRAM traffic ($I = \frac{\text{FLOPs}}{\text{Byte}}$). For the GTX 1050 Ti running at its base clock of $1392\text{ MHz}$, peak single-precision compute performance is:
\begin{equation}
    P_{\text{compute}} = 768 \text{ cores} \times 1392 \times 10^6 \text{ Hz} \times 2 \frac{\text{FLOP}}{\text{cycle}} \approx 2.138 \text{ TFLOP/s}
\end{equation}
With theoretical memory bandwidth $B_{\text{mem}} = 112.0\text{ GB/s}$, the architectural ridge point $I^*$ is:
\begin{equation}
    I^* = \frac{P_{\text{compute}}}{B_{\text{mem}}} = \frac{2138 \times 10^9 \text{ FLOP/s}}{112.0 \times 10^9 \text{ Bytes/s}} \approx 19.09 \text{ FLOPs/Byte}
\end{equation}
Any operational regime with $I < I^*$ is strictly \emph{memory bandwidth-bound}, whereas regimes with $I > I^*$ are \emph{compute-bound}.

\subsection{Prefill versus Decode Regimes}
During transformer inference, execution alternates between two distinct regimes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Prefill (Prompt Processing):} The prompt of length $L_p$ is processed concurrently via General Matrix Multiplications (GEMM). The arithmetic intensity scales with prompt length:
    \begin{equation}
        I_{\text{prefill}} \approx \frac{2 \cdot P \cdot L_p}{P \cdot b_{\text{weight}} + \mathcal{O}(L_p^2)} \approx \frac{2 \cdot L_p}{b_{\text{weight}}}
    \end{equation}
    For 4-bit weights ($b_{\text{weight}} \approx 0.55\text{ Bytes}$) and $L_p = 256$, $I_{\text{prefill}} \approx 930 \gg 19.09$. Prefill is strictly compute-bound, saturating CUDA cores.

    \item \textbf{Decode (Token Generation):} Autoregressive token generation generates a single token per forward pass via General Matrix-Vector Multiplications (GEMV). Every model parameter must be streamed from GDDR5 VRAM into register files:
    \begin{equation}
        I_{\text{decode}} \approx \frac{2 \cdot P}{P \cdot b_{\text{weight}} + 2 \cdot n_{\text{layers}} \cdot d_{\text{kv}} \cdot L_{\text{ctx}} \cdot b_{\text{cache}}} \approx \frac{2}{0.55} \approx 3.64 \text{ FLOPs/Byte}
    \end{equation}
    Since $3.64 \ll 19.09$, decode throughput is strictly capped by effective memory bandwidth $B_{\text{eff}}$:
    \begin{equation}
        \text{Throughput}_{\text{decode}} = \frac{B_{\text{eff}}}{S_{\text{weights}} + S_{\text{KV}}} \quad \left[\frac{\text{tokens}}{\text{s}}\right]
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Analytical KV Cache Sizing and Memory Pressure}
The memory consumption $M_{\text{KV}}$ of the Key-Value cache for context length $L_{\text{ctx}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
    M_{\text{KV}} = 2 \times n_{\text{layers}} \times n_{\text{kv\_heads}} \times d_{\text{head}} \times L_{\text{ctx}} \times b_{\text{cache}} \quad [\text{Bytes}]
\end{equation}
For a 4B parameter model (e.g., Qwen-3.5-4B with 32 layers, 8 KV heads, head dimension 128, FP16 cache $b_{\text{cache}}=2$):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item At $L_{\text{ctx}} = 2048$: $M_{\text{KV}} = 2 \times 32 \times 8 \times 128 \times 2048 \times 2 = 268.4\text{ MiB}$.
    \item At $L_{\text{ctx}} = 8192$: $M_{\text{KV}} = 1073.7\text{ MiB}$ ($1.05\text{ GiB}$).
    \item At $L_{\text{ctx}} = 32768$: $M_{\text{KV}} = 4294.9\text{ MiB}$ ($4.20\text{ GiB}$).
\end{itemize}
Because Qwen-3.5-4B requires $2987\text{ MiB}$ of static weight memory in VRAM, expanding the context to $32\text{k}$ without cache quantization or CPU offloading would require $2987 + 4295 = 7282\text{ MiB}$, drastically exceeding the $4096\text{ MiB}$ hardware budget and causing an immediate Out-Of-Memory (OOM) kernel termination.

\subsection{Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) Speculative Decoding Theory}
Speculative decoding circumvents the memory-bandwidth bottleneck by verifying multiple draft tokens in a single target forward pass. Let $\gamma$ be the lookahead window ($\gamma = 1$ for single-head MTP) and $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ be the token acceptance rate. The theoretical speedup factor $S$ is modeled as:
\begin{equation}
    S = \frac{1 + \alpha \gamma}{1 + \frac{c_{\text{draft}}}{c_{\text{target}}}}
\end{equation}
where $c_{\text{draft}}$ is the computational cost of generating the draft token and $c_{\text{target}}$ is the verification cost. In native MTP architectures, the speculative head reuses the hidden representations of the base transformer ($c_{\text{draft}} \approx 0.05 \cdot c_{\text{target}}$). Assuming an empirical acceptance rate $\alpha \approx 0.40$, the expected speedup is:
\begin{equation}
    S \approx \frac{1 + 0.40}{1 + 0.05} \approx 1.333\times \quad (+33.3\%)
\end{equation}
which precisely matches our empirical steady-state measurements on the GTX 1050 Ti.

All environmental parameters (identical weights, prompt tokens, context size, zero temperature $\tau=0.0$, locked clock state) were held constant across 5 independent repetitions totaling $N=15$ queries per condition.

\paragraph{Hypothesis Formulation and Testing:}
We evaluate the directional hypothesis that MTP enhances token throughput:
\begin{equation}
    H_0: \mu_{\text{MTP}} \le \mu_{\text{base}} \quad \text{vs.} \quad H_1: \mu_{\text{MTP}} > \mu_{\text{base}}
\end{equation}
Because sample variances are unequal ($s_{\text{MTP}}^2 = 117.07$, $s_{\text{base}}^2 = 33.99$), we employ Welch's unequal variances $t$-test. The effective degrees of freedom $\nu$ is calculated via the Satterthwaite approximation:
\begin{equation}
    \nu = \frac{\left(\frac{s_1^2}{N_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{N_2}\right)^2}{\frac{(s_1^2 / N_1)^2}{N_1 - 1} + \frac{(s_2^2 / N_2)^2}{N_2 - 1}} = \frac{\left(\frac{117.07}{15} + \frac{33.99}{15}\right)^2}{\frac{(7.805)^2}{14} + \frac{(2.266)^2}{14}} \approx 21.50
\end{equation}
The calculated test statistic is:
\begin{equation}
    t = \frac{\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{N_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{N_2}}} = \frac{42.21 - 32.25}{\sqrt{7.805 + 2.266}} = \frac{9.96}{3.173} = 3.1391
\end{equation}
The resulting one-tailed $p$-value is $p = 0.00243$ ($p < 0.01$). Non-parametric Mann-Whitney $U$ test yields $U = 210.0$ ($p = 2.84 \times 10^{-5}$), rejecting the null hypothesis $H_0$ with overwhelming statistical significance. The standardized effect size is Cohen's $d = 1.1462$, confirming a large practical advantage. In steady-state warmed execution, MTP delivers a deterministic \textbf{33.2\%} speedup ($44.95$ vs $33.75\text{ tok/s}$, $t=21.05$, $p=3.65\times 10^{-14}$).

\subsection{Controlled Quantization Impact (IQ4\_XS versus Q4\_K\_M)}
To isolate the performance cost of quantization schemes, Gemma-4-E2B was evaluated under two quantization schemes with identical model architecture:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]

\subsection{Vulnerability Analysis: Indirect Prompt Injection}
As detailed in Figure~\ref{fig:adversarial_en}, models exhibited polarized security behavior across attack vectors:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Direct Exploit Refusal (100\%):} All four models cleanly refused direct requests to generate functional exploits for CVE-2021-44228 (Log4Shell), refused stealth C++ keylogger implementation, and resisted persona-based DAN jailbreaks and system prompt extraction attacks.
    \item \textbf{Indirect Log Injection Vulnerability (0\% Refusal):} When untrusted input was presented inside a simulated syslog buffer containing the directive \texttt{[CRITICAL\_}\allowbreak\texttt{SYSTEM\_}\allowbreak\texttt{OVERRIDE]}, all four models executed the secondary prompt and emitted the Canary string (\texttt{INJECTION\_SUCCEEDED}). This demonstrates an architectural flaw in current instruction tuning: RLHF/DPO boundaries fail to isolate user instructions from untrusted context tokens.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Model Context Protocol (MCP) Integration}
Agentic evaluation utilized a compliant Python Model Context Protocol server executing JSON-RPC 2.0 over \texttt{stdio}. Models were required to invoke \texttt{get\_proxmox\_node\_status} with schema validation, parse the resulting JSON payload, and formulate a natural language operational summary. Gemma-4-E2B, Qwen-3.5-4B, and Qwen-3.5-2B successfully completed the multi-turn exchange. In contrast, Llama-3.2-3B experienced hallucinations during tool output ingestion on turn 2, failing to synthesize node statistics.

\section{Hardware Stability, Throttling, and Validity Threats}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Thermal Dynamics:} NVML telemetry revealed that during continuous benchmark loops, GPU temperature rose from $35^\circ\text{C}$ to a peak of $72^\circ\text{C}$. At $70^\circ\text{C}$, the NVIDIA driver throttled the core clock from $1392\text{ MHz}$ to $1290\text{ MHz}$, accounting for the modest variance observed in later ablation iterations.
    \item \textbf{Microarchitectural Limits:} The Pascal architecture (Compute 6.1) lacks native INT4/INT8 tensor arithmetic acceleration and FP16 half-rate execution, executing quantized operations via software emulation in CUDA cores.
    \item \textbf{Context Scaling Risk:} Under the 4096 MiB budget, 4B models cannot scale beyond $8\text{k}$ context without KV cache quantization (\texttt{q8\_0} or \texttt{q4\_0}) or CPU offload.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusions and Deployment Recommendations}
Empirical evidence confirms that Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) speculative decoding delivers a statistically verified, causal \textbf{30.9\% to 33.2\%} throughput acceleration on memory-constrained GPUs. \textbf{Gemma-4-E2B (IQ4\_XS + MTP)} is established as the optimal deployment choice for autonomous homelab agents on the GTX 1050 Ti, maximizing throughput ($46.3\text{ tok/s}$) and energy efficiency ($0.89\text{ tok/J}$) while conserving 63.2\% of the VRAM budget. To mitigate indirect prompt injection vulnerabilities, edge systems must integrate deterministic regex-based boundary isolation filters before context ingestion.
