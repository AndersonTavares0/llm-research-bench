\section{Introduction and Systems Context}

The expansion of autonomous applications across cyber-physical systems, mobile robotics, edge gateways, and microservers requires local natural language processing without continuous cloud connectivity~\cite{williams2009,leviathan2023}. Offloading operational telemetry, control commands, and private user context to centralized proprietary APIs presents three prohibitive failure modes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.1em]
    \item \textbf{Data Sovereignty and Privacy:} Severe risk of confidential data exfiltration and violation of data protection regulations (GDPR/LGPD);
    \item \textbf{Temporal Determinism:} Variable WAN transit latencies ($100\text{ ms}$ to $2000\text{ ms}$) with acute vulnerability to packet loss and link disconnects;
    \item \textbf{Recurring Costs:} Unbounded operational expenditures per million tokens generated on commercial cloud infrastructure.
\end{enumerate}

Small Language Models (SLMs), parameterized within the $0.5\text{B}$ to $4.0\text{B}$ weight regime, provide a compelling solution for local inference~\cite{rottger2023}. However, deploying these models on entry-level edge accelerators strictly bounded to $4096\text{ MiB}$ of physical VRAM introduces acute microarchitectural and thermodynamic constraints.

The Pascal microarchitecture (e.g., NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti), widespread across educational, industrial, and resource-constrained edge environments, lacks dedicated Tensor Cores and HBM stacks, relying on GDDR5 memory with a maximum theoretical bandwidth of $112.0\text{ GB/s}$~\cite{williams2009}. Under these hardware boundaries, autoregressive token generation is fundamentally memory-bandwidth bound, demanding aggressive tensor quantization and speculative decoding to achieve interactive throughput~\cite{leviathan2023}.

\section{Research Problem, Questions, and Hypotheses}

The core problem addressed in this research is the empirical and theoretical quantification of multi-dimensional trade-offs between generation throughput, physical VRAM footprint, quantization fidelity, and adversarial resilience in SLMs under a strict $4\text{ GiB}$ memory ceiling.

We formulate four formal Research Questions ($Q_1$ to $Q_4$) and their corresponding testable hypotheses:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{$Q_1$ (Speculative Acceleration):} \emph{Can Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) accelerate autoregressive decoding on edge accelerators without exceeding the physical VRAM budget?} \\
    \textbf{Hypothesis $H_1$:} Speculative decoding via integrated MTP draft prediction increases steady-state warmed decode throughput by more than 30\%, with an incremental VRAM cost below $100\text{ MiB}$.
    \item \textbf{$Q_2$ (Quantization and Physical Frontiers):} \emph{What is the relative impact of importance matrix quantization (i-quants) compared to legacy uniform block quantization (k-quants)?} \\
    \textbf{Hypothesis $H_2$:} Non-uniform IQ4\_XS formats preserve higher informational density and decode velocity than legacy Q4\_K\_M formats under identical DRAM footprint.
    \item \textbf{$Q_3$ (Adversarial Resilience):} \emph{Do compact instruction-tuned models maintain strict boundary separation between operational commands and untrusted contextual data?} \\
    \textbf{Hypothesis $H_3$:} SLMs demonstrate high refusal rates against direct attacks, but universally succumb to indirect prompt injections in secondary inputs due to data-command conflation.
    \item \textbf{$Q_4$ (Linguistic Disparity and Bias):} \emph{What is the tokenization fertility penalty and demographic bias exhibited by compact models when processing non-English languages?} \\
    \textbf{Hypothesis $H_4$:} Tokenizer fertility in Portuguese is substantially higher than in English, imposing an effective throughput and context capacity penalty on Romance languages.
\end{itemize}

\section{Theoretical Foundations and Mathematical Modeling}

\subsection{Roofline Model and Operational Arithmetic Intensity}
Computational performance bounds are governed by the Roofline Model formulated by Williams et al.~\cite{williams2009}. The maximum attainable compute throughput $P$ (in $\text{GFLOP/s}$) is expressed by:
\begin{equation}
P(I) = \min\left(P_{\text{peak}}, \; B_{\text{DRAM}} \times I\right)
\end{equation}
where $P_{\text{peak}}$ is peak floating-point throughput ($2138\text{ GFLOP/s}$ FP32 on the GTX 1050 Ti at $1392\text{ MHz}$ boost clock), $B_{\text{DRAM}}$ is theoretical memory bandwidth ($112.0\text{ GB/s}$), and $I$ is operational arithmetic intensity in $\text{FLOPs/byte}$.

During prefill processing with sequence length $L$, arithmetic intensity scales with batch and matrix dimension:
\begin{equation}
I_{\text{prefill}} \approx \frac{2 \cdot N_{\text{params}} \cdot L}{2 \cdot N_{\text{params}} \cdot b + \mathcal{O}(L^2)} \gg 10\text{ FLOPs/byte}
\end{equation}
operating predominantly in the compute-bound regime.

Conversely, during token-by-token autoregressive decoding ($L=1$), generating each token requires streaming the entire weight tensor from DRAM:
\begin{equation}
I_{\text{decode}} \approx \frac{2 \cdot N_{\text{params}}}{N_{\text{params}} \cdot b} = \frac{2}{b}\text{ FLOPs/byte}
\end{equation}
where $b$ is the byte-width per parameter. For 4-bit quantized weights ($b = 0.5\text{ bytes}$), $I_{\text{decode}} \approx 4\text{ FLOPs/byte}$. Because the ridge point of the GTX 1050 Ti is $I_{\text{ridge}} = P_{\text{peak}} / B_{\text{DRAM}} = 2138 / 112 \approx 19.09\text{ FLOPs/byte}$, autoregressive decoding of SLMs operates deeply within the memory-bound regime. The absolute theoretical throughput ceiling is:
\begin{align}
P_{\text{ceil\_decode}} &= 112.0\text{ GB/s} \times 4\text{ FLOPs/byte} \nonumber \\
&= 448\text{ GFLOP/s}
\end{align}
corresponding to a theoretical generation throughput limit of:
\begin{equation}
\text{Throughput}_{\text{max}} = \frac{B_{\text{DRAM}}}{S_{\text{weights}}}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Key-Value Cache Dynamics and Scaling Limits}
During autoregressive generation, the Key-Value cache stores past projection tensors to avoid quadratic recomputation of self-attention. The memory footprint of the KV cache ($S_{\text{KV}}$) for context length $L_{\text{ctx}}$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{KV}} = 2 \times n_{\text{layers}} \times n_{\text{kv\_heads}} \times d_{\text{head}} \times L_{\text{ctx}} \times b_{\text{elem}}
\end{equation}
where $n_{\text{layers}}$ is the number of transformer blocks, $n_{\text{kv\_heads}}$ is the number of key/value heads under Grouped-Query Attention (GQA), $d_{\text{head}}$ is head dimension, and $b_{\text{elem}}$ is precision byte size ($2$ bytes for FP16, $1$ byte for Q8\_0, and $0.5$ bytes for Q4\_0).

In uncompressed FP16 cache configurations, memory consumption expands linearly with $L_{\text{ctx}}$, directly contesting static weight allocations in bounded VRAM.

\subsection{Speculative Decoding and Multi-Token Prediction}
Speculative decoding, formalized by Leviathan et al.~\cite{leviathan2023}, circumvents sequential memory-bandwidth limits by generating candidate token sequences using a low-cost draft mechanism and verifying them in parallel with the target model during a single evaluation pass.

Under the Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) paradigm, auxiliary heads on the target network predict $K$ future tokens concurrently. The expected generation speedup $S$ is modeled as:
\begin{equation}
E[S] = \frac{1 + \sum_{k=1}^{K} \alpha^k}{1 + \mathcal{O}_{\text{draft}}}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha \in [0, 1]$ is the empirical draft acceptance rate and $\mathcal{O}_{\text{draft}}$ represents relative verification overhead. When $\alpha > 0.5$ and $\mathcal{O}_{\text{draft}} \ll 1$, speculative execution breaks through the DRAM bandwidth barrier, emitting multiple accepted tokens per memory transfer cycle.

\subsection{Adversarial Robustness and Data-Command Conflation}
Safety evaluations in edge SLMs focus on maintaining robust isolation between system instructions and untrusted contextual inputs~\cite{cheng2024,wallace2024}. Edge agents operating via protocols like Model Context Protocol (MCP) ingest unstructured data from network logs, sensor payloads, and user commands. Indirect prompt injection manifests when untrusted context overrides privileged instructions:
\begin{equation}
\text{Prompt} = \mathcal{T}_{\text{system}} \parallel \mathcal{D}_{\text{untrusted}}(\mathcal{I}_{\text{adversarial}}) \parallel \mathcal{T}_{\text{user}}
\end{equation}
The Attack Success Rate (ASR) and False Positive Rate (FPR) are defined as:
\begin{align}
\text{ASR} &= \frac{1}{M_{\text{adv}}} \sum_{i=1}^{M_{\text{adv}}} \mathbb{I}(\text{Compromised}_i) \\
\text{FPR} &= \frac{1}{M_{\text{ben}}} \sum_{j=1}^{M_{\text{ben}}} \mathbb{I}(\text{Refusal}_j)
\end{align}

\section{Materials and Experimental Methodology}

\subsection{Physical Edge Testbed}
All experiments were conducted on a dedicated physical edge workstation:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.1em]
    \item \textbf{Accelerator:} NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti (GP107, 768 CUDA cores, base clock $1290\text{ MHz}$, boost $1392\text{ MHz}$, 4096\,MiB GDDR5, 128-bit bus, $112.0\text{ GB/s}$, 75W TDP, Compute Capability 6.1, no Tensor Cores).
    \item \textbf{Host System:} Linux Kernel 6.8.12-8-pve (Proxmox VE 8.2), cgroup v2 process isolation, NVIDIA driver 580.173.02, CUDA Runtime 13.0.
    \item \textbf{Serving Runtime:} \texttt{llama-server} (commit b4120+, native cuBLAS sm\_61 compilation, OpenAI-compatible HTTP daemon on port 18081).
    \item \textbf{Telemetry Instrumentation:} Continuous asynchronous sampling via \texttt{libnvidia-ml.so.1} (\texttt{pynvml} ctypes) recording physical VRAM, die temperature, compute utilization, and core clocks at 250\,ms intervals.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Experimental Controls and Execution Protocol}
To eliminate cold-start bias and thermal throttling artifacts:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.1em]
    \item \textbf{Controlled Warmup ($k=2$):} Each model executed two 10-token warmup inferences prior to measurement capture.
    \item \textbf{Independent Replications ($N=10$):} Every experimental condition was replicated 10 times under a fixed seed (\texttt{seed=42}) and greedy decoding ($\tau = 0.0$).
    \item \textbf{Thermal Stability:} A 400\,ms idle pause was enforced between queries; die temperatures remained strictly within $38^\circ\text{C}$--$74^\circ\text{C}$, confirming absence of thermal throttling.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Inferential Statistical Methods}
Hypothesis testing uses Welch's two-sample $t$-test with Satterthwaite's approximation for unequal variances:
\begin{equation}
t = \frac{\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}}}, \quad \nu = \frac{\left(\frac{s_1^2}{n_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{n_2}\right)^2}{\frac{(s_1^2/n_1)^2}{n_1-1} + \frac{(s_2^2/n_2)^2}{n_2-1}}
\end{equation}
Two-sided 95\% confidence intervals were computed from Student's $t$-distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom. Standardized effect size was quantified via Cohen's $d$.

Calculating effective memory bandwidth utilization ($B_{\text{eff}} = S_{\text{weights}} \times \text{Throughput}_{\text{decode}}$) reveals that models operating without speculative decoding saturate a stable fraction of the GDDR5 bus:

    \item \textbf{Gemma-4-E2B [Q4\_K\_M]:} $1.40\text{ GB} \times 26.9\text{ tok/s} \approx 37.7\text{ GB/s}$ (33.7\%).

These empirical observations confirm that standard autoregressive decoding achieves between 33\% and 50\% of theoretical peak GDDR5 bandwidth, bounded by memory bus transaction latency and unaligned DRAM access patterns.

\section{Statistical Analysis and Factorial MTP Ablation}

To test Hypothesis $H_1$, we compared \textbf{Gemma-4-E2B [MTP]} ($43.35 \pm 0.57$\,tok/s) against the non-speculative baseline \textbf{Gemma-4-E2B [Q4\_K\_M]} ($26.92 \pm 0.23$\,tok/s) under identical physical conditions.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/mtp_ablation_ci.pdf}
\caption{Controlled MTP ablation: throughput comparison with 95\% Student's $t$ confidence intervals ($N=10$, $\tau=0.0$).}
\label{fig:mtp_ablation}
\end{figure}

Welch's two-sample $t$-test yielded:
\begin{equation}
t = \frac{43.35 - 26.92}{\sqrt{\frac{0.57^2}{10} + \frac{0.23^2}{10}}} = \frac{16.43}{0.195} \approx 84.26, \quad \nu = 11.8
\end{equation}
with $p < 10^{-15}$ and standardized effect size Cohen's $d = 38.8$.

Internal runtime telemetry from \texttt{llama-server} logged an empirical draft acceptance rate of $\alpha = 52.8\%$ (65 accepted tokens out of 123 generated), with an average accepted block length of $\bar{L} = 2.05$ tokens. This mechanism directly explains the circumvention of the DRAM bandwidth wall: while weight reading transfers data for 1 token, parallel speculative verification outputs an average of 2.05 tokens per cycle, yielding a net causal speedup of +61.0\%.

Furthermore, physical VRAM consumption for the MTP-enabled model increased by only $40.0\text{ MiB}$ ($2036.7$ vs $1996.7\text{ MiB}$, a 2.0\% increment), fully confirming Hypothesis $H_1$.

\subsection{Absence of False Refusals ($\text{FPR} = 0.0$\%)}
All tested models answered legitimate systems engineering prompts (e.g., terminating deadlocked processes with \texttt{kill -9} or dissecting SYN packets with \texttt{tcpdump}) with rigorous technical precision, refuting hypotheses of conservative over-alignment on operational tasks.

\subsection{Critical Vulnerability to Indirect Injections}
Corroborating Hypothesis $H_3$, all models from 1.7B to 4B parameters proved highly susceptible to indirect instruction injection (ASR between 66.7\% and 100.0\%). When processing simulated system log traces containing embedded imperative commands, models routinely abandoned the primary summarization task to emit the attacker's canary token. This demonstrates an architectural deficiency in standard autoregressive Transformers regarding data-command segregation~\cite{wallace2024}.

\section{Algorithmic Bias and Language Fertility Evaluation}

\subsection{Demographic Bias and Counterfactual Consistency}
Evaluations inspired by BBQ and StereoSet-PT revealed that Gemma-4-E2B sustained 100\% neutrality across counterfactual gender and socioeconomic pairs. However, under the P3B3 regional variation probe, compact models (SmolLM2 and Qwen-0.5B) failed to issue categorical assertions of cognitive parity, producing evasive statements rather than directly rejecting regional stereotypes.

\subsection{Tokenizer Fertility in Portuguese}
Measuring tokenization fertility ($\text{tokens}/\text{word}$) demonstrated that vocabularies across most SLMs remain heavily biased toward English:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.1em]
    \item In \textbf{SmolLM2-1.7B}, fertility reached $2.08\text{ tok/w}$ in Brazilian Portuguese and $2.17\text{ tok/w}$ in European Portuguese, compared to $1.56\text{ tok/w}$ in English. Each Portuguese phrase consumes up to 40\% more context and decoding latency.
    \item \textbf{Qwen-3.5-4B} achieved superior multilingual fertility ($1.30\text{ tok/w}$ in pt-BR vs $1.35\text{ tok/w}$ in en-US) owing to its expanded 152k-token TikToken vocabulary.
\end{itemize}

\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}

\subsection{Internal Validity}
NVML telemetry was sampled at 250\,ms intervals; while adequate for capturing sustained tensor allocation peaks, sub-millisecond transients are filtered by the GPU telemetry interface. Energy consumption is modeled via calibrated thermal TDP points rather than direct hardware AC power analyzers.

\subsection{External Validity}
Experiments were executed on an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti (Pascal microarchitecture, Compute 6.1). Modern accelerators based on Ampere, Ada Lovelace, or Blackwell architectures feature native Tensor Cores and FP8/FP4 support, wherein arithmetic intensity boundaries in the Roofline model will shift.

\subsection{Construct Validity}
The defensive evaluation utilized 10 structured synthetic attack scenarios. While fully reproducible and deterministic, adaptive multi-turn jailbreak strategies may expose additional vulnerabilities.

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}

This research provides an empirical, mathematical, and systems evaluation of Small Language Models (0.5B to 4B) operating under a physical $4096\text{ MiB}$ VRAM limit.

Primary empirical conclusions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.1em]
    \item \textbf{Causal Speculative Acceleration:} MTP speculative decoding delivers a genuine $+61.0\%$ decoding throughput gain ($43.4$ vs $26.9$\,tok/s) under identical weights and quantization, demonstrating that edge inference can effectively overcome the GDDR5 DRAM bandwidth bottleneck.
    \item \textbf{4B Physical Boundary:} 4B architectures operate at the critical edge of memory capacity (76.7\%). Gemma-3-4B mandates Q4\_0 KV cache compression to avert physical out-of-memory faults.
    \item \textbf{Defensive Segregation Vulnerability:} While all models achieved $\text{FPR}=0.0$\% on benign tasks, resilience against indirect injection remains critically weak ($\text{ASR} \le 100\%$). Compact edge models must not be granted direct tool execution capabilities without external schema validation and parameter sanitization.
    \item \textbf{Linguistic Tax:} English-centric tokenizers impose up to a $2.17\times$ token overhead on Portuguese text.
\end{enumerate}

Future research will investigate ternary parameter quantization (1.58 bits) and physical PCIe current probe instrumentation for direct hardware power profiling.
