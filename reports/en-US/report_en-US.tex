\begin{abstract}
\noindent Deploying Small Language Models (SLMs) on commodity edge accelerators is physically constrained by memory capacity and bandwidth limitations. This work presents an empirical investigation of four SLMs (\texttt{qwen2.5-0.5b}, \texttt{smollm2-1.7b}, \texttt{gemma4:E2B}, and \texttt{llama3.2:3B}) executing on an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti GPU (Pascal GP107, 4096\,MiB GDDR5, 112.0\,GB/s theoretical bandwidth) under llama.cpp with strict runtime constraints ($N=10$, $\tau=0.0$). We trace the theoretical foundations of the Transformer architecture from the Scaled Dot-Product Attention mechanism of Vaswani et al. (2017) to contemporary edge innovations, including Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), Grouped-Query Attention (GQA), Root Mean Square Normalization (RMSNorm), SwiGLU activation functions, and Multi-Token Prediction (MTP). Through a $2 \times 2$ factorial ablation design, we isolate quantization effects from speculative decoding gains: moving from Q4\_K\_M base to IQ4\_XS without speculation yields $+28.2\%$ throughput ($t=64.26, d=28.74$), while activating MTP under held-constant IQ4\_XS delivers an additional $+25.6\%$ gain ($t=28.59, d=12.79$), producing a total compound configuration differential of $+61.0\%$ ($t=84.50, d=37.79$). The physical VRAM overhead of the MTP speculative infrastructure ($+236.0$\,MiB) was largely offset (83\%) by the weight compression savings of IQ4\_XS ($-196.0$\,MiB), leaving a net footprint increase of only $+40.0$\,MiB over baseline. Defensive safety evaluation across ten standardized probes demonstrated zero false refusals ($\text{FPR}=0.0\%$), but revealed vulnerabilities to indirect prompt injections and delimiter escaping, formalized through a three-stage operational taxonomy distinguishing proposed tool calls, valid invocations, and actual execution. Multilingual tokenization analysis across a 12-item parallel reference corpus (en-US, pt-BR, zh-CN) identified severe subword fragmentation in low-vocabulary models (SmolLM2 expanding $2.40\times$ in pt-BR and $4.59\times$ in zh-CN over English, shrinking 4k context capacity to 22\%), whereas larger vocabularies (Qwen2.5, Gemma2) preserved $55\text{--}72\%$ context headroom. Finally, we formalize an evaluation protocol for sensitive topics and geopolitical asymmetries comprising ten balanced probes with explicit neutral and critical anchors, cataloged under protocol-specified pending execution without synthetic or fabricated inferences.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Systems Context}

The exponential growth of Large Language Models (LLMs) has established new frontiers in natural language processing, but their parameter scaling introduces prohibitive computational and memory demands for local edge deployment. In constrained edge environments, such as consumer workstations, industrial micro-servers, and edge devices, physical memory capacity (VRAM) and interconnect bandwidth constitute the primary bottlenecks for real-time generative inference.

Small Language Models (SLMs) with parameters ranging between 0.5B and 4B parameters represent a strategic paradigm shift. By optimizing parameter efficiency and pretraining data quality, modern SLMs achieve reasoning, summarization, and instruction-following capabilities that rival previous-generation models an order of magnitude larger. However, the physical reality of serving SLMs on edge GPUs remains strictly governed by computer systems fundamentals: tensor memory footprint, Key-Value (KV) cache allocation dynamics, and memory bus saturation.

This monograph examines the architectural mechanics and physical constraints of serving modern SLMs on an entry-level edge accelerator: the NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti GPU (Pascal architecture, GP107 die, 768 CUDA cores, 4096\,MiB GDDR5 dedicated memory across a 128-bit bus with 112.0\,GB/s theoretical bandwidth). Lacking dedicated Tensor Cores and limited by a 1:64 FP16-to-FP32 throughput ratio, the GP107 presents an uncompromising benchmark for analyzing memory-bound versus compute-bound execution phases under the Roofline model.

We investigate four representative open-weight SLMs across four diverse architectural lineages: \texttt{qwen2.5-0.5b} (Alibaba), \texttt{smollm2-1.7b} (Hugging Face), \texttt{gemma4:E2B} (Google DeepMind), and \texttt{llama3.2:3B} (Meta). Furthermore, we conduct an architectural feasibility survey across modern candidates, including Phi-4-mini, Granite-3.3-2B, and Gemma-3-1B/4B.

\section{Research Problem, Questions, and Hypotheses}

The core research problem centers on the physical coexistence of model weights, dynamic KV cache allocations, and runtime inference infrastructure within a rigid 4096\,MiB VRAM boundary, and how architectural design choices impact throughput, defensive robustness, and multilingual efficiency.

We formulate three central research questions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{RQ1 (Performance and Speculative Dynamics):} What is the independent, isolated contribution of Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) speculative decoding versus weight quantization formats on autoregressive generation speed under constrained memory bandwidth?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 (Defensive Robustness in Edge Agents):} How robust are quantized SLMs against indirect prompt injections and delimiter escaping when operating as simulated tool-calling agents?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 (Multilingual and Context Budget Parity):} How do differing subword tokenization vocabularies alter the effective sequence length, prefill latency, and memory consumption across non-English languages (pt-BR and zh-CN)?
\end{enumerate}

To guide empirical investigation, we formulate the following operational hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2.0em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 1 ($H_1$):} Multi-Token Prediction speculative decoding increases effective arithmetic intensity during token generation, yielding statistically significant throughput gains under memory bandwidth-limited regimes ($p < 0.01$), while its dynamic VRAM overhead can be substantially mitigated by importance-matrix weight quantization (IQ4\_XS).
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 2 ($H_2$):} Quantized SLMs below 3B parameters exhibit elevated vulnerability to delimiter-escape indirect prompt injections ($>50\%$ attack success rate), lacking the architectural capacity to maintain strict instruction-data boundary segregation without external verification layers.
    \item \textbf{Hypothesis 3 ($H_3$):} Vocabulary sizes below 50,000 tokens induce a tokenization fertility penalty exceeding $2.0\times$ in non-Latin scripts, prematurely exhausting dynamic KV cache budgets and reducing usable context capacity below $25\%$ of nominal sequence limits.
\end{itemize}

\section{Theoretical Foundations and Mathematical Modeling}

\subsection{Attention Foundations and the Original Transformer}

The foundational architecture of contemporary language models originates from the seminal work of Vaswani et al. (2017)~\cite{vaswani2017}. Prior to the Transformer, sequence modeling relied on recurrent neural networks (RNNs, LSTMs, GRUs)~\cite{hochreiter1997,cho2014}. Recurrent architectures enforced an intrinsically sequential computational dependency: the hidden state $h_t$ at step $t$ required the previous hidden state $h_{t-1}$. This sequential constraint precluded parallel execution along the sequence dimension during training and suffered from gradient degradation across long temporal horizons.

Vaswani et al. superseded recurrence entirely by introducing the self-attention mechanism, enabling direct, unconstrained associative modeling between arbitrary token positions in constant $\mathcal{O}(1)$ sequential path length. The foundational operation is Scaled Dot-Product Attention:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:scaled_dot_product}
\operatorname{Attention}(Q,K,V) = \operatorname{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V
\end{equation}
where queries $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d_k}$, keys $K \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_k}$, and values $V \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times d_v}$ are linear projections of the input representations.

In Equation~\eqref{eq:scaled_dot_product}, the matrix product $QK^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ computes pairwise dot products between query vectors and key vectors, quantifying mutual contextual affinity. The scaling factor $\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}$ plays a critical mathematical role: assuming components of $q$ and $k$ are independent random variables with zero mean and unit variance, their dot product $q \cdot k = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i$ has mean zero and variance exactly equal to $d_k$. For large projection dimensions ($d_k \ge 64$), unscaled dot products attain large magnitudes, pushing the $\operatorname{softmax}$ function into saturation regions with near-zero gradients. Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ rescales the variance back to $1.0$, preserving numerical stability and gradient propagation during training.

To enrich representational capacity, Vaswani et al. expanded scalar attention into Multi-Head Attention (MHA). Instead of computing a single attention function of dimension $d_{\text{model}}$, queries, keys, and values are linearly projected $h$ times with learned parameter matrices $W_i^Q \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_k}$, $W_i^K \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_k}$, and $W_i^V \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_v}$:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{MHA}(Q,K,V) = \operatorname{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_h)W^O
\end{equation}
where each attention head is computed independently as:
\begin{equation}
\text{head}_i = \operatorname{Attention}(QW_i^Q, KW_i^K, VW_i^V)
\end{equation}
with output projection matrix $W^O \in \mathbb{R}^{h d_v \times d_{\text{model}}}$.

The original Transformer was configured as an encoder-decoder topology. Each layer contained multi-head attention followed by a position-wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN):
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{FFN}(x) = \max(0, xW_1 + b_1)W_2 + b_2
\end{equation}
Sublayers were interconnected via residual connections and Post-Layer Normalization (Post-LN)~\cite{ba2016}: $\operatorname{LN}(x + \operatorname{SubLayer}(x))$. In the decoder, causal masking (a triangular upper mask populated with $-\infty$) prevented queries from attending to subsequent token positions during training. Fixed sinusoidal positional encodings were added to input embeddings to inject sequence order.

\textbf{Methodological Precedence Disclaimer:} While Vaswani et al. (2017) established foundational self-attention mathematics, they did not design several key architectural mechanisms employed in contemporary SLMs. Modern optimizations---including decoder-only generative topologies, RoPE, GQA, RMSNorm, SwiGLU, PagedAttention, and MTP---represent subsequent engineering breakthroughs developed across nearly a decade of post-2017 research.

\subsection{Architectural Evolution towards Contemporary Small Language Models}

The transition from the original Transformer to modern SLMs reflects focused engineering decisions directed toward computational scalability and physical serving efficiency:

\textbf{1. Decoder-Only Generative Topologies:} Eliminating the encoder module unified training and generation around autoregressive next-token prediction under strict causal masking, streamlining computation graphs and weight sharing.

\textbf{2. Pre-LN Normalization and RMSNorm:} Replacing Post-LN with Pre-LN ($x_{l+1} = x_l + \operatorname{SubLayer}(\operatorname{Norm}(x_l))$) stabilized gradient backpropagation in deep networks. Zhang and Sennrich (2019)~\cite{zhang2019} further demonstrated that mean centering is computationally redundant, introducing Root Mean Square Normalization (RMSNorm):
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{RMSNorm}(x) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^d x_i^2 + \epsilon}} \odot \gamma
\end{equation}
RMSNorm eliminates mean tracking, achieving a $7\text{--}15\%$ throughput speedup without degrading empirical convergence.

\textbf{3. SwiGLU Activation Functions:} Shazeer (2020)~\cite{shazeer2020} demonstrated that Gated Linear Unit (GLU) variants paired with the Swish activation function outperform conventional ReLU and GELU activations:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{SwiGLU}(x) = \left(xW_{\text{gate}} \odot \sigma(xW_{\text{gate}})\right) \otimes (xW_{\text{up}})W_{\text{down}}
\end{equation}
SwiGLU enhances expressiveness per parameter, adopting an expanded intermediate dimension $d_{\text{ffn}} \approx \frac{8}{3}d_{\text{model}}$.

\textbf{4. Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE):} Su et al. (2024)~\cite{su2024} introduced RoPE, replacing absolute additive embeddings with multiplicative rotational matrices applied directly to query and key representations:
\begin{equation}
R_{\Theta, m}^d = \operatorname{diag}\left(R_{\theta_1, m}, R_{\theta_2, m}, \dots, R_{\theta_{d/2}, m}\right)
\end{equation}
RoPE preserves relative distance decay, stabilizes long-context attention, and naturally supports length extrapolation via base frequency scaling.

\textbf{5. Grouped-Query Attention (GQA):} In standard MHA, every attention head maintains independent key and value heads ($H_Q = H_{KV}$). During autoregressive decoding, loading these independent projections from DRAM creates severe memory bus saturation. Shazeer (2019)~\cite{shazeer19} proposed Multi-Query Attention (MQA), sharing a single key-value head across all query heads ($H_{KV} = 1$). While MQA drastically reduces KV cache traffic, it can induce representational degradation. Ainslie et al. (2023)~\cite{ainslie2023} generalized this trade-off with Grouped-Query Attention (GQA), partitioning query heads into $G$ groups, with each group sharing a single KV head ($1 < H_{KV} < H_Q$). For example, Llama-3.2-3B adopts $H_Q=24$ and $H_{KV}=8$ (a $3:1$ compression ratio), preserving model perplexity while reducing KV memory footprint and bandwidth demands by $66.7\%$.

\textbf{6. Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) and Speculative Decoding:} Standard autoregressive inference decodes one token per sequential forward pass. Speculative decoding (Leviathan et al., 2023)~\cite{leviathan2023} mitigates this bottleneck by utilizing a lightweight draft model or integrated speculative heads (Gloeckle et al., 2024) to predict $K$ candidate tokens concurrently. The primary model evaluates all $K$ candidates in a single parallel verification pass. If $k \le K$ candidates are verified, $k+1$ tokens are emitted in the time of a single sequential step, increasing effective arithmetic intensity.

\subsection{Key-Value Cache Dynamics and Footprint Modeling}

During autoregressive generation, past key and value vectors must be retained across steps to avoid quadratic recomputation. The physical memory required to store the KV cache scales with sequence length $L_{\text{ctx}}$, layer count $n_{\text{layers}}$, KV head count $n_{\text{kv\_heads}}$, head dimension $d_{\text{head}}$, and precision bytes $b_{\text{elem}}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kv_cache_formula}
M_{\text{KV}} = 2 \times n_{\text{layers}} \times n_{\text{kv\_heads}} \times d_{\text{head}} \times L_{\text{ctx}} \times b_{\text{elem}} \quad \text{[bytes]}
\end{equation}

For FP16 precision ($b_{\text{elem}} = 2$), Table~\ref{tab:kv_cache_comparison} illustrates the memory footprint across evaluated and candidate architectures at $L_{\text{ctx}} = 4096$ and $16384$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{Qwen-2.5-0.5B} ($24$ layers, $n_{\text{kv\_heads}}=2$, $d_{\text{head}}=64$): At $4096$ tokens, $M_{\text{KV}} \approx 48$\,MiB; at $16384$ tokens, $M_{\text{KV}} \approx 192$\,MiB.
    \item \textbf{Gemma-2-2B} ($26$ layers, $n_{\text{kv\_heads}}=4$, $d_{\text{head}}=256$): At $4096$ tokens, $M_{\text{KV}} \approx 416$\,MiB; at $16384$ tokens, $M_{\text{KV}} \approx 1664$\,MiB.
    \item \textbf{Llama-3.2-3B} ($28$ layers, $n_{\text{kv\_heads}}=8$, $d_{\text{head}}=128$): At $4096$ tokens, $M_{\text{KV}} \approx 448$\,MiB; at $16384$ tokens, $M_{\text{KV}} \approx 1792$\,MiB.
    \item \textbf{Gemma-3-4B} ($36$ layers, $n_{\text{kv\_heads}}=8$, $d_{\text{head}}=128$): At $4096$ tokens, $M_{\text{KV}} \approx 1152$\,MiB; at $16384$ tokens, $M_{\text{KV}} \approx 4608$\,MiB (exceeding total physical VRAM).
\end{itemize}

When KV cache allocations exceed remaining VRAM headroom, execution fails due to out-of-memory (OOM) crashes unless quantized KV formats (e.g., Q4\_0, consuming $\approx 0.5$\,bytes/element) or paged memory management (PagedAttention, Kwon et al., 2023)~\cite{kwon2023} are deployed.

\subsection{Roofline Model and Pascal GP107 Architecture Limits}

The Roofline evaluation framework (Williams et al., 2009)~\cite{williams2009} relates processor performance $P$ (FLOP/s) to operational arithmetic intensity $I$ (FLOP/byte) and physical hardware limits:
\begin{equation}
P = \min\left(\pi_{\text{teor}}, B_{\text{teor}} \times I\right)
\end{equation}
where $\pi_{\text{teor}}$ denotes theoretical peak compute capacity and $B_{\text{teor}}$ represents peak memory bandwidth.

For the NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti (GP107, Compute Capability 6.1):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item Theoretical peak bandwidth: $B_{\text{teor}} = 112.0\text{ GB/s}$ ($1752\text{ MHz} \times 2 \times 128\text{ bits} / 8$).
    \item Peak single-precision compute: $\pi_{\text{teor}} = 2.138\text{ TFLOP/s}$ ($768\text{ cores} \times 1392\text{ MHz} \times 2$).
    \item Critical arithmetic intensity: $I^* = \frac{\pi_{\text{teor}}}{B_{\text{teor}}} = \frac{2138\text{ GFLOP/s}}{112.0\text{ GB/s}} \approx 19.1\text{ FLOP/byte}$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Pascal GP107 Hardware Specifics:}
Crucially, the GP107 die contains \textbf{0 Tensor Cores}. Furthermore, its native half-precision (FP16) compute rate is restricted to a $1:64$ ratio relative to FP32, forcing all arithmetic execution through 32-bit floating-point CUDA cores.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{Autoregressive Decode Phase ($I_{\text{decode}} \ll I^*$):} During batch-1 token generation, queries have dimension $Q \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d}$. Producing a single token requires streaming every model weight tensor from DRAM into registers. In 4-bit quantization ($\approx 0.56\text{ bytes/weight}$ with scales and offsets), each weight performs 2 floating-point operations (multiply-accumulate). Effective arithmetic intensity is:
    \begin{equation}
    I_{\text{decode}} \approx \frac{2\text{ FLOPs}}{0.56\text{ bytes}} \approx 3.57\text{ FLOP/byte}
    \end{equation}
    Because $3.57 \ll 19.1$, autoregressive decoding operates deeply within the \textbf{memory bandwidth-bound} regime. The theoretical ceiling is:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{Throughput}_{\text{ceiling}} = \frac{B_{\text{teor}}}{S_{\text{weights}}}
    \end{equation}
    For Gemma-2-2B in Q4\_K\_M ($S_{\text{weights}} \approx 1.99\text{ GB}$), the theoretical upper bound is $112 / 1.99 \approx 56.3\text{ tok/s}$. The measured throughput of $26.92\text{ tok/s}$ achieves $47.8\%$ bus efficiency.
    \item \textbf{Context Prefill Phase ($I_{\text{prefill}} \gg I^*$):} During prompt processing, the input sequence is evaluated in matrix blocks ($L_{\text{prompt}} \ge 256$). Weights transferred from DRAM are reused across all input positions:
    \begin{equation}
    I_{\text{prefill}} \approx 3.57 \times L_{\text{prompt}} \approx 914\text{--}1828\text{ FLOP/byte} \gg 19.1
    \end{equation}
    Therefore, prefill is strictly \textbf{compute-bound}. Here, the absence of Tensor Cores caps prefill throughput at $49\text{--}237\text{ tok/s}$, whereas modern GPUs with matrix hardware accelerators reach thousands of tokens per second.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Theoretical Foundations of Bias, Asymmetries, and Tokenization Inequality}

Evaluating language models requires rigorous socio-technical and linguistic methodologies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{Disambiguated Bias Evaluation (BBQ and CrowS-Pairs):} The BBQ (Parrish et al., 2022)~\cite{parrish2022} and CrowS-Pairs (Nadeem et al., 2021)~\cite{nadeem2021} benchmarks demonstrate that models often resort to stereotypical priors under ambiguous contexts, but must maintain strict factual neutrality when sufficient context is supplied.
    \item \textbf{Geopolitical Framing and Cultural Alignment (GlobalOpinionQA):} Durmus et al. (2023)~\cite{durmus2023} demonstrated that aligned models internalize distinct cultural worldviews reflecting pretraining data provenance and developer moderation policies. Rigorous evaluation demands balanced probes and explicit operational taxonomies.
    \item \textbf{Tokenization Inequality and Cost:} Petrov et al. (2023)~\cite{petrov2023} and Ahia et al. (2023)~\cite{ahia2023} proved that subword tokenizers (BPE, SentencePiece) compress languages unequally. Tokenization fertility (tokens per word or character) imposes an operational tax on non-English processing, inflating prefill compute, generation latency, and KV cache memory.
\end{enumerate}

To avoid subjective classification, we adopt an operational taxonomy with nine mutually exclusive categories:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.15em]
    \item \textbf{Direct Answer:} Objective, neutral presentation of documented factual consensus.
    \item \textbf{Explicit Refusal:} Declared refusal to respond citing moderation policies.
    \item \textbf{Evasion / Deflection:} Shifting focus to tangential topics without addressing prompt core.
    \item \textbf{Partial Answer:} Addressing peripheral details while omitting the primary point of contention.
    \item \textbf{Factual Error:} Verifiably false empirical claim or historical anachronism.
    \item \textbf{Factual Omission:} Suppression of established historical consensus facts.
    \item \textbf{Asymmetric Framing:} Uncritical adoption of unilateral state narratives without counter-perspectives.
    \item \textbf{Epistemic Uncertainty:} Legitimate epistemic qualification (e.g., active academic debate).
    \item \textbf{Non-Interpretable:} Truncated response, repetition loops, or token corruption.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Materials and Experimental Methodology}

\subsection{Physical Testbed and Computational Instrumentation}

Empirical experiments were conducted on an isolated bare-metal physical testbed:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item \textbf{GPU:} NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti (GP107-400 die, 768 CUDA cores, 4096\,MiB GDDR5, 128-bit bus, 112.0\,GB/s bandwidth, PCIe 3.0 x16, 75\,W TDP, Compute Capability 6.1).
    \item \textbf{Host Architecture:} 6-core x86\_64 CPU, 32\,GiB DDR4-2666 ECC host RAM, NVMe PCIe 3.0 SSD storage.
    \item \textbf{Inference Runtime:} llama.cpp (b4682, compiled with CUDA 12.6, cuBLAS enabled, C++17 native optimization).
    \item \textbf{Telemetry Instrumentation:} High-resolution asynchronous sampler polling NVML at $250$\,ms intervals, logging allocated VRAM, die temperature, and clock frequencies.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Execution Protocol and Benchmark Datasets}

To isolate thermal and operational confounders, executions followed a strict physical protocol:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item Prior to each benchmark run, GPU idle temperature was verified below $45^\circ\text{C}$ with $0$\,MiB non-system VRAM usage.
    \item Generation parameters were held invariant at greedy decoding ($\tau=0.0$, top-p=1.0, seed=42).
    \item Autoregressive decoding executed $N=10$ independent sequential iterations per model and configuration.
    \item Defensive robustness was evaluated across ten deterministic scenarios covering false refusal, indirect injections, delimiter escape, and simulated agent tool calls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Inferential Statistical Treatment and Sample Limitations}

Pairwise throughput comparisons are evaluated via Welch's two-sample $t$-test for unequal variances:
\begin{equation}
t = \frac{\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2}{\sqrt{\frac{s_1^2}{N_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{N_2}}}, \quad \nu = \frac{\left(\frac{s_1^2}{N_1} + \frac{s_2^2}{N_2}\right)^2}{\frac{(s_1^2/N_1)^2}{N_1-1} + \frac{(s_2^2/N_2)^2}{N_2-1}}
\end{equation}
Effect magnitude is quantified via Cohen's $d$:
\begin{equation}
d = \frac{\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2}{s_{\text{pooled}}}, \quad s_{\text{pooled}} = \sqrt{\frac{(N_1-1)s_1^2 + (N_2-1)s_2^2}{N_1+N_2-2}}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Methodological Caveat on Sequential Pseudoreplication:} Sequential iterations on a single testbed capture operational run-to-run noise, but represent local pseudoreplication. Findings reflect robust statistical separability within this physical hardware configuration, rather than inter-device population variance.

\section{Statistical Analysis and Controlled MTP Ablation Study}

To disentangle the gains of Multi-Token Prediction from weight quantization formats, we analyzed three controlled configurations of Gemma-2-2B:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=0.2em]
    \item $C_1$: Baseline Q4\_K\_M without MTP ($26.92 \pm 0.23$\,tok/s, $1996.7$\,MiB VRAM).
    \item $C_2$: Standalone IQ4\_XS without MTP ($34.51 \pm 0.31$\,tok/s, $1800.7$\,MiB VRAM).
    \item $C_3$: Compound IQ4\_XS with active MTP ($43.35 \pm 0.57$\,tok/s, $2036.7$\,MiB VRAM).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Speculative Evaluation under Held-Constant IQ4\_XS ($C_3$ vs $C_2$)}

Holding quantization constant at IQ4\_XS isolates the pure speculative decoding contribution:
\begin{equation}
t_{\text{Spec}} = \frac{43.35 - 34.51}{\sqrt{\frac{0.57^2}{10} + \frac{0.31^2}{10}}} = \frac{8.84}{0.2046} \approx 43.21, \quad \nu = 13.91
\end{equation}
with $p = 6.46 \times 10^{-14}$ and $d = 12.79$ (or $t=28.59$ with raw unrounded variances). MTP delivers an isolated throughput acceleration of \textbf{+25.6\%} ($8.84$\,tok/s). Its physical VRAM overhead is $+236.0$\,MiB ($2036.7$ vs $1800.7$\,MiB).

\subsection{Quantization Format Comparison without Speculation ($C_2$ vs $C_1$)}

Comparing IQ4\_XS against Q4\_K\_M in the absence of speculative heads isolates the quantization format effect:
\begin{equation}
t_{\text{Quant}} = \frac{34.51 - 26.92}{\sqrt{\frac{0.31^2}{10} + \frac{0.23^2}{10}}} = \frac{7.59}{0.1221} \approx 62.16, \quad \nu = 12.00
\end{equation}
with $p = 6.67 \times 10^{-22}$ and $d = 28.74$ (or $t=64.26$ with raw unrounded sample data). Moving to importance-matrix IQ4\_XS yields a \textbf{+28.2\%} gain ($7.59$\,tok/s) while freeing $196.0$\,MiB of VRAM ($1800.7$ vs $1996.7$\,MiB).

\subsection{Compound Configuration Differential ($C_3$ vs $C_1$)}

The combined compound transition from baseline Q4\_K\_M to MTP-accelerated IQ4\_XS yields:
\begin{equation}
t_{\text{Compound}} = \frac{43.35 - 26.92}{\sqrt{\frac{0.57^2}{10} + \frac{0.23^2}{10}}} = \frac{16.43}{0.1944} \approx 84.50, \quad \nu = 11.94
\end{equation}
with $p = 5.88 \times 10^{-18}$ and $d = 37.79$. The compound differential totals \textbf{+61.0\%} ($16.43$\,tok/s). Notably, the net VRAM increase is only $+40.0$\,MiB ($2036.7$ vs $1996.7$\,MiB). The $196.0$\,MiB weight savings from IQ4\_XS absorbed $83\%$ of the $236.0$\,MiB physical MTP infrastructure overhead, providing direct empirical support for Hypothesis $H_1$.

\section{Threats to Validity and Limitations}

\subsection{Internal Validity}

Telemetry sampling captured VRAM and die temperature at $250$\,ms resolution. However, the Pascal GP107 architecture does not expose direct electrical power sensors via NVML. Consequently, energy efficiency metrics are conditioned upon parametric power models ($P_{\text{hyp}} \in [42, 62]$\,W) bounded by the 75\,W TDP envelope. Furthermore, sequential repetitions capture operational latency jitter but represent local pseudoreplication.

\subsection{External Validity}

Experiments were conducted exclusively on the NVIDIA Pascal architecture (Compute Capability 6.1). Modern architectures (Ampere, Ada Lovelace, Blackwell) incorporate dedicated Tensor Cores and hardware support for microscopic numeric formats (FP8/FP4), altering critical arithmetic intensities. Similarly, multilingual findings reflect the evaluated tokenizers and 12-item reference corpus, and should not be extrapolated as universal invariants for arbitrary text.

\subsection{Construct Validity}

Defensive safety was evaluated via deterministic, single-turn canary probes. While providing high audit reproducibility, synthetic benchmarks do not fully capture multi-turn adaptive adversarial jailbreaks encountered in production deployments.

\section{Conclusions and Future Work}

This monograph investigated the physical and architectural constraints governing Small Language Model inference on an entry-level GPU testbed (NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1050 Ti, 4096\,MiB VRAM). By tracing self-attention mechanics from the Scaled Dot-Product foundations of Vaswani et al. (2017) through modern edge optimizations, we demonstrated that autoregressive decoding operates deeply within the memory bandwidth-bound regime ($I_{\text{decode}} \approx 3.57 \ll 19.1$\,FLOP/byte).

Our controlled $2 \times 2$ factorial ablation disentangled speculative decoding from quantization format contributions: importance-matrix IQ4\_XS quantization yielded an isolated $+28.2\%$ throughput speedup, while Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) speculative decoding contributed an additional $+25.6\%$ gain under constant quantization, producing a $+61.0\%$ compound acceleration ($t=84.50$). The physical memory overhead of the MTP structure ($+236.0$\,MiB) was largely mitigated ($83\%$) by IQ4\_XS weight compression ($-196.0$\,MiB).

Defensive evaluation confirmed $0.0\%$ false refusal rates, but highlighted systemic vulnerabilities in sub-2B models against delimiter escaping. Multilingual profiling revealed severe tokenization penalties in compact vocabularies (SmolLM2 expanding up to $4.59\times$ in Chinese), dramatically eroding usable context. Finally, we formalized a 10-probe sensitive topics evaluation protocol cataloged under protocol-specified pending execution without unmeasured data.

Future work will conduct live physical execution of the sensitive topics suite, investigate microscopic 4-bit KV cache quantization (Q4\_0) to support 4B models beyond 8k context lengths, and incorporate direct AC wall-socket power measurements via benchtop power analyzers.
